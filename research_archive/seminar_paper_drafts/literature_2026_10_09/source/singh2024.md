# Singh (2024): administrative-data integrity and digital testing

**Citation and publication verification.** Abhijeet Singh. “Improving Administrative Data at Scale: Experimental Evidence on Digital Testing in Indian Schools.” *The Economic Journal* 134(661), July 2024, 2207–2223. DOI: [10.1093/ej/ueae017](https://doi.org/10.1093/ej/ueae017). [Oxford Academic published article](https://academic.oup.com/ej/article/134/661/2207/7625080). **Summary and relevance (reserve).** Singh studies a field experiment covering more than 2,400 schools in Andhra Pradesh. Government officials administered otherwise comparable grade-4 assessments on paper or tablets; an independently proctored retest in 117 schools supplied an external benchmark. Teacher-administered paper tests overstated achievement by 16–20 percentage points relative to the retest, and 38–43% of paper-test classrooms were flagged by an item-pattern procedure, compared with 2–5% of tablet classrooms. Tablet results generally matched the independent retest. The paper also validates the scalable flagging procedure against direct audits. This is a useful measurement precedent for separating an operational recorded outcome from independently observed status and for documenting provenance and coverage. It does **not** show that the current NFE-departure records were manipulated, or that absence-label error follows the same process.
