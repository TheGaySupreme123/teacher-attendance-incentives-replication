Track A replication plus extension

Duflo teacher attendance: from the original 21 pp effect to score-gap closing.

The original experiment asks whether camera monitoring plus attendance-linked pay got teachers to come to school. The extension asks whether that bundled treatment especially reduced absenteeism among lower-scoring teachers, or more precisely, whether it flattened the link between baseline teacher score and attendance.

Gap closing, not over-performance

The effect is larger for low-score teachers mainly because their control attendance starts lower.

Read this as: treatment does not make low-score teachers outperform high-score teachers. It nearly removes the high-low attendance gap seen in control schools.

Teacher-score gradient by school

In control schools, attendance rises with baseline teacher score. In treatment schools, that slope is nearly flat.

When does the score divergence appear?

The high-score effect is stable; the low-score effect is especially large after the post-test.

Timing is exploratory. It helps frame what to investigate further, not a separate randomized mechanism.

Paper claim ladder

Use the stronger claims first and keep weaker or non-identified interpretations out of the headline.

Strong The bundled camera-plus-incentive treatment raises teacher attendance. The replicated all-sample effect is +20.7 percentage points and rounds to the original +0.21.
Stronger extension Control schools show a teacher-score attendance gradient; treatment schools show a much flatter relationship.
Suggestive The below-median group has a larger point-estimate treatment effect than the above-median group, but the binary interaction p-value is 0.105.
Avoid Do not claim a separate camera-only or incentive-only effect from this reduced-form contrast. The randomized treatment bundles both.

What to look into further

These are the next checks that would make the Track A paper more defensible.

Mechanism Explain why lower-score control teachers had lower attendance before interpreting the larger treatment effect.
Timing Check why the low-score effect is largest after the post-test and whether this aligns with camera/payroll implementation details.
Robustness Keep month or block fixed-effect stability checks close to the main estimate so the extension does not look like data mining.
Learning Treat student outcomes as secondary unless the score heterogeneity can be connected cleanly to instructional time.
